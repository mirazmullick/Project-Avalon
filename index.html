Project Avalon

A seven-month rollout across B2B, B2C, and B2E, built to turn a 35-year agency's trust into digital market share. B2B carries the first six to seven months of revenue and proof. B2C builds anticipation in parallel without paid acquisition. B2E follows once B2B has real results to show.

Why this order

B2B first, B2C in waiting, B2E once proven

B2B produces fewer, larger wins faster, using a warm agent network that already trusts the parent brand. B2C needs a mature product and real word of mouth before it deserves paid spend, so months one through seven build anticipation rather than chase strangers. B2E waits until month four, when B2B case studies exist to make the pitch credible.

Brand communication & management

One brand, three audiences, one system

Avalon is carrying a 35-year agency's reputation online for the first time. That reputation is the asset, and the brand system exists to protect it while it stretches across three very different audiences.

One identity, applied everywhere

The same visual identity, palette, and wordmark appear on every B2B deck, every B2C social post, and every B2E pitch document. No touchpoint should ever look like it belongs to a different company than the one an agent or a customer already trusts.

Same voice, calibrated by audience

B2B communication is direct and data-led, since travel managers and agency owners want proof, not persuasion. B2C is warm and story-led, built around the narrative of a legacy agency going digital. B2E is credible and outcome-focused, built around one signed pilot rather than broad claims. The words change, the brand underneath does not.

A single point of ownership

Brand and digital sign-off sits with one owner rather than being split across teams. Every piece of public-facing content is checked against a single style guide before it ships, weekly for fast-moving channels like paid social, monthly for slower ones like case studies and PR placements.

Protecting inherited trust

Every public message is measured against a simple test: does this match how the legacy agency would show up in person. Complaints and negative reviews get a same-day response protocol, because early trust online compounds or breaks fast in Bangladesh's OTA market.

Marketing coordinated with the agency floor

B2B messaging is coordinated directly with the agency's existing relationship managers, not sent blind. What a sub-agent hears from marketing matches what they hear from the contact they already know, so the digital push reinforces the relationship instead of competing with it.

The process

Brand visibility, awareness, and word-of-mouth conversion

Each segment moves through the same three stages, but the tactic underneath is different every time, because a corporate travel manager, a retail traveler, and an HR head don't discover trust the same way.

B2B
Brand visibility

Agent portal presence inside the parent agency's existing network. One consistent brand identity across every deck, portal screen, and one-pager agents see.

Awareness

LinkedIn targeting corporate travel managers and agency owners. Monthly webinars plus a two-city roadshow, Dhaka then Chattogram.

Word-of-mouth conversion

A referral commission for agents who bring in other agents. Agents already talk to agents daily, this turns that conversation into acquisition.

B2C
Brand visibility

A teaser landing page live from month one, waitlist only. Light, consistent content so the brand feels familiar by the time it launches.

Awareness

Influencer seeding built on story, a legacy agency going digital, not product features. Two PR pushes: platform announcement, then launch.

Word-of-mouth conversion

A referral-gated waitlist, refer three friends to move up the list. Curiosity becomes unpaid distribution instead of paid reach.

B2E
Brand visibility

The same brand identity, translated into a corporate one-pager and a case study document once B2B has real wins to show.

Awareness

Direct outreach to 15 to 20 target companies, HR and admin heads, not marketing. Sales-led, minimal media spend.

Word-of-mouth conversion

One signed pilot becomes the reference case for the next prospect. Peer companies refer once there is a result to point to.
